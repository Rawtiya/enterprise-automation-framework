# Enterprise Automation Principles

## 1. Business Problem First

Automation should begin with a clearly defined business problem or opportunity.

Technology should not be the reason for automating a process.

---

## 2. Eliminate Before Automating

Unnecessary activities should be eliminated before technology is introduced.

> Do not automate waste.

---

## 3. Simplify Before Digitizing

Reduce unnecessary steps, approvals, handoffs, and complexity before introducing digital solutions.

---

## 4. Standardize Before Scaling

A process should be sufficiently standardized before automation is replicated across teams, locations, or business units.

---

## 5. Select Technology Based on the Problem

Technology should be selected based on process characteristics, business requirements, risk, and expected value.

Potential solutions include:

- Workflow
- BPM
- RPA
- APIs
- Intelligent Document Processing
- AI / ML
- Generative AI
- Process Mining
- Analytics

---

## 6. Design for Exceptions

Automation should explicitly address:

- Normal flow
- Exceptions
- Escalations
- Manual intervention
- Failure recovery

---

## 7. Measure What Matters

Automation performance should be measured using business outcomes rather than automation activity alone.

Examples:

- Cost
- Cycle time
- Quality
- Capacity
- Customer experience
- Risk
- Compliance

---

## 8. Build Controls Into the Process

Security, privacy, compliance, auditability, and operational controls should be considered during solution design.

---

## 9. Ownership Must Be Clear

Every production automation should have accountable business and technical ownership.

---

## 10. Benefits Must Be Realized

Going live is not the definition of success.

Success means the expected business outcome is achieved and sustained.

---

## 11. Reuse Before Rebuild

Before creating a new automation, assess whether an existing component, integration, workflow, business rule, or capability can be reused.

---

## 12. Scale What Works

Solutions should be considered for enterprise replication only after their value, reliability, controls, and sustainability have been demonstrated.

---

## 13. Continuously Improve

Automation should become part of the continuous improvement cycle.

> Measure → Analyze → Improve → Standardize → Scale

---

## Executive Principle

> **The goal is not maximum automation. The goal is maximum sustainable business value.**
